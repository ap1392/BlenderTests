**High confidence reading of a design document; moderate confidence applicable to built heights.** The proposed roof and lower stair differ visibly from the finished building. Do not copy those earlier forms. Nevertheless, these height labels are stronger evidence than the current assumed 4.2 m story.

The 2013 office pod differs from later plans.

## Roof and ceiling

[University sustainability signage PDF](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.northeastern.edu/dist/3/787/files/2023/08/ISEC-Sustainable-Flyer-Full.pdf), saved `sustainability.pdf`, has an undimensioned skylight section on page 2 (`skylight_section.png`). It shows sloping glazing above a deep asymmetric tapered lightwell. [Payette's lighting article](https://www.payette.com/in-the-news/northeastern-isec-featured-in-arch-record/) also describes three cone-shaped apertures.

Photo `isec_reflections_024.jpg` clearly shows a rounded triangular lower opening, smaller offset upper opening, rectilinear glazing grid, smooth white taper and sparse seams. Vertical elliptical rings cannot reproduce this. Loft two closed rounded triangular splines and incline the upper glazing; fit their proportions against this photo. Photos 011 and 026 show the three apertures along the atrium, with the largest central opening above the stair. Perspective prevents a reliable simple diameter ratio.

Photos 011 and 023 show thin parallel oak blades, black perpendicular mounting rails, and a dark recessed strip before the white balcony fascia. Blades follow one direction within each field and are trimmed to its curved edge. Do not make every field radial or give all adjacent fields one global direction without checking photos.

## Laboratory curtainwall

Photo `isec_reflections_018.jpg` is nearly frontal. The blue-gray spandrel occupies about 31% of a floor cycle: in the 1923×1280 preview, the middle band spans y744–896 (152 px), while the story spans roughly 487 px. At 4.4196 m, start with a 1.38 m opaque zone and 3.04 m clear zone, then refine in the matched camera. The present 0.78 m band is too thin. Central vertical bays are approximately 174 px wide, suggesting 1.58 m bays; mullions are slim, around 40–60 mm by visual estimate.

A fine white horizontal frit pattern occupies roughly the lower 0.65–0.8 m of each clear zone. This is translucent and distinct from the solid blue spandrel. Glass strongly reflects the opposite white balconies while still revealing the interior; scene brightness and real contents are essential to matching it.

Visible layers: atrium glass, dark carpeted write-up desks with pale wood dividers and blue chairs, another clear partition roughly 3–4 m farther back, then bright white laboratory benches and shelving with lime green accents. Large white ceiling rafts and linear pendant lights contrast with dark beams and gray rectangular columns. These shallow visible layers can be modeled without inventing accessible laboratories.

## Balconies, bridges and ground level

Photo 023 is a close view of an end bridge. White slab fascia has a soft underside chamfer/recess. Glass guards rise directly above the slab and support a stainless handrail on small circular standoffs; seams are around 1 m apart by visual estimate. Groups of three adjustable white spotlights sit under balcony junctions. The bright lime wall includes exit doors and the visible room range “360–379”. The end bridge is a wide furnished lounge with full-height exterior glazing, not a plain narrow crossing.

Photo 011 shows many varied-diameter white drum lights beneath the laboratory wing, round concrete columns, glazed classroom fronts, pale oak standing counters and broad sweeping gray bands in the ground floor. The present sparse lights and uniform floor miss major visible features.

## Bounded roof placement follow-up

The central lower mouth appears roughly 1.6 times the apparent width of the highest stair turn in photo 011, despite being farther from the camera. A roughly 6 m stair therefore suggests a central mouth around 9–11 m rather than 4–6 m. The smaller upper glazed aperture might be 6–7 m. End mouths are plausibly 5.5–7 m. These are weak perspective estimates for initial fitting only.

Starting centers consistent with the current published-plan boundary are west `(-12,3.3)`, center `(0,4)`, east `(12,8)`, in the existing origin convention. The evidence does not support claiming these as triangulated positions. A matched camera must establish the rotation of each rounded triangle; a world-space angle cannot be recovered honestly by looking at unregistered photographs alone. The uncertainty is specifically position/orientation, not whether the openings are tapered rounded triangles.

A useful silhouette constraint for photo 011's fully visible far opening: in the 1917×1279 preview, its approximate perimeter is `(830,288),(858,276),(906,273),(963,282),(983,311),(984,346),(963,397),(940,402),(887,375),(847,342),(830,313)`. This identifies the visible rounded triangular mouth in image space. It can be back-projected to the roof plane once the camera is registered. The nearby large opening is cropped at the top and unsuitable for a closed contour fit in that image.

Photo 023 strongly verifies parallel straight slats within the gallery/bridge field. Existing blades that fan with each local gallery normal should become a fixed-direction clipped field. Allow a discrete direction change between architectural fields only when a reference supports it.
