## Identification and direction

The shared published-plan +X end of ISEC faces Columbus Avenue. This follows the architect's site plan and the 2013 BCDC landscape plan: laboratory bar on the rail-to-street axis, pedestrian bridge at the opposite end, Columbus Avenue beyond the recessed end lounge. The view is therefore toward the opposite residential frontage, not toward the railway or parking garage.

Its official photo shows the same red-brown brick, pale lintel/sill bands and white sash windows visible beyond ISEC's glass. The address match is probable, not definitively established by a labeled photograph. Model this as the documented Columbus residential frontage, with probable address noted in metadata.

## Defensible massing

The landscape plan suggests roughly **45–55 m from the recessed lounge glass to the opposite façade**, including the setback and street. This is an inferred pixel-ratio estimate using the known approximate laboratory-wing length; it is not a surveyed distance. At current lounge glass x≈21.5, a starting façade plane near **x≈70–75 m** is defensible. Its front faces toward −X. Adjust lateral position from the actual reference view rather than adding a whole city block.

Use one closed shallow building volume, five stories, red-brown brick with a pale parapet/cornice. A nominal height around **17–19 m** (five ordinary residential floors) is an architectural estimate; only the five-story count is verified. Give the volume enough depth to remain coherent from neighboring cameras, but do not invent roof structures or rear detail. Width and exact depth are unresolved; model only enough of the visibly continuous frontage, approximately six to eight window bays, rather than claiming the full building has that count.

## Visible façade grid

The ISEC image shows **at least three complete stacked window rows**, with another partial upper row behind reflected ISEC structure. Across the unobscured pane group there are roughly six to eight visible window bays, several partly hidden by ISEC mullions and reflections. These are partial visible counts, not the building's total grid.

Important features visible in the crop:

- Tall rectangular white sash/window frames, with a horizontal meeting rail.
- Some windows grouped into wider assemblies; avoid one identical narrow aperture everywhere.
- Pale lintels slightly wider than the openings and pale sill/string-course bands.
- Red-brown brick piers between windows, no modern all-glass curtainwall.
- Regular floor-to-floor rhythm, shallow relief and a flat roof line.

A plausible initial module is 3–3.5 m floor spacing and 2.5–3 m bay pitch, with window openings around 1.2–1.5 m wide and 1.7–2 m high. These metric values are explicitly inferred modeling seeds, not measured specifications. Use the photo to refine screen-space proportions.

Keep materials naturally warm; the cyan cast in the reference comes primarily through the ISEC glass and reflections. Do not color the brick blue to reproduce the transmitted photograph. No surrounding towers are needed to replace the blank sky in this view.
